## HandBrake preset analysis

Two HandBrake presets were inspected read-only:

- `1080p 6db gain.json` — the normal/default preset.
- `2160p with 6db gain.json` — the less frequently used 4K preset.

### Shared behavior

- MP4 output using VideoToolbox HEVC (`vt_h265`).
- Peak frame rate is set to 60 fps rather than preserving a strict constant 60 fps.
- One 5.1 AC-3 audio encode at 224 kb/s with **+6 dB gain**.
- First matching audio track is selected; the 1080p preset limits automatic selection to English while the 2160p preset has no audio-language filter.
- AAC, AC-3, E-AC-3, and DTS are permitted for passthrough by the copy mask, with Core Audio AAC as the fallback encoder.
- Chapter markers and metadata are preserved.
- Automatic cropping is disabled and the presets allow upscaling to their maximum dimensions.

### Important subtitle difference

- The normal 1080p preset uses `SubtitleTrackSelectionBehavior: none` and `SubtitleBurnBehavior: none`. It therefore does not automatically select or burn an English forced track.
- The 2160p preset uses `SubtitleTrackSelectionBehavior: first` and `SubtitleBurnBehavior: first`. It can burn the first English subtitle, but “first English” is not the same as “forced English”; this can select a complete dialogue or SDH track instead.
- Both presets have HandBrake's foreign-audio search disabled.

This configuration accounts for subtitle selection being inconsistent with the desired result.

## Recommended subtitle policy for SwiftyTranscoder

The app should treat **forced English subtitles** and **full English subtitles** as different concepts, and show the exact selected track before conversion.

### Default: English forced dialogue

1. Inspect every English subtitle track rather than choosing the first one.
2. Prefer a track whose Matroska disposition is `forced`.
3. Also recognize clear title labels such as `Forced`, `English Forced`, or `Foreign Parts Only`; titles should supplement the disposition flag, not replace it blindly.
4. If a forced track is found, select it and burn it into the video by default.
5. If multiple forced-English candidates exist, prefer a non-SDH track, show the candidates, and require an unambiguous selection instead of guessing.
6. If no track is marked forced, analyze subtitle packet count and on-screen duration to identify a likely short “foreign parts only” track. Present it as **Likely forced — please confirm**, rather than silently burning it.

The present samples include one excellent test case: `Reacher - s04e08 - Cut.mkv` has an English subtitle titled `American English [Forced]` with both `default` and `forced` dispositions. The app should automatically choose and burn stream 2 for this file, while not confusing stream 3 (`American English [SDH]`) with the forced track.

### Foreign-language film: full English subtitles

When the primary audio language is not English:

1. Offer the best full English subtitle track prominently.
2. Prefer ordinary English over English SDH unless the user chooses SDH.
3. Preselect **Burn in full English subtitles**, but show the decision clearly before conversion.
4. Keep an easy alternative to include the English track as selectable MP4 subtitles when its format supports that choice.
5. If the source subtitle is image-based (PGS/VobSub), either burn it directly or use a separately confirmed OCR workflow; do not silently convert it as though it were text.

### Suggested simple UI

Show one subtitle decision per job:

- **Forced English found — burn in** (automatic default).
- **Foreign-language audio — burn full English** (recommended, user-changeable).
- **No forced English identified** (nothing burned, with a subtitle picker available).
- **Possible forced track — confirmation needed** (used when metadata is missing or contradictory).

The conversion summary should always display the selected stream number, language, title, forced/SDH status, and whether it will be burned or retained as a selectable track. This makes the behavior predictable and auditable instead of depending on track order.

## Interpretation notes

- `ffprobe` can reliably identify codec families, channels, layouts, and HDR/color signaling that is exposed in the container and elementary stream.
- Atmos and DTS:X detection is not guaranteed. Atmos may be carried as metadata inside TrueHD or E-AC-3, and some files do not label it in a way `ffprobe` exposes. Entries marked “not explicitly identified” require a deeper bitstream inspection or confirmation with MediaInfo before the transcoder treats Atmos as absent.
- A missing per-stream bitrate is normal in Matroska and does not mean the stream has no bitrate; the container may only expose an overall bitrate.
- For the proposed app, retain original audio when compatible, create a dependable stereo AAC track for Plex clients, and make +6 dB gain an explicit optional accessibility setting with clipping protection.
